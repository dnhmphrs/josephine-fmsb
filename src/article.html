Mapping the Transatlantic–Pacific AI Security Policy Field

A comparative reading of how policy communities in Europe, the United States, and China frame the security dimensions of advanced AI — and where their assumptions quietly diverge.

Authors Josephine Shen
Daniel Humphries
Published 2026
Reading time ~22 min
Cite as Shen & Humphries (2026)
Abstract

We survey the published positions of thirty-one policy organisations working on artificial intelligence and security across three jurisdictions. Coding each group's output along axes of threat model, preferred instrument, and locus of authority, we find that apparent transatlantic consensus on "AI safety" masks substantial disagreement about what is being secured and against whom.
